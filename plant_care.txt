COMPLETE PLANT CARE KNOWLEDGE BASE
Covers general care rules, 25 popular plants (watering, sunlight, soil, maintenance, problems, uses, safety), pest control, seasonal care and troubleshooting.
Medicinal uses listed are traditional or commonly reported uses, not medical advice. Consult a doctor before using any plant for health purposes.


=====================================================
PART 1: GENERAL PLANT CARE BASICS
=====================================================

GENERAL WATERING RULES: Check the soil with your finger. Water when the top 2-3 cm of soil feels dry for most houseplants. Water deeply until water drains from the bottom hole, then empty the saucer. Overwatering kills more plants than underwatering. Water in the early morning or evening, not in harsh afternoon sun. Use room-temperature water. Succulents and cacti need water only when soil is completely dry. Ferns, tulsi and leafy herbs prefer evenly moist soil.

SIGNS OF OVERWATERING: Yellow leaves, soft or mushy stems, black or brown wet-looking spots, a sour smell from the soil, fungus gnats, and root rot. Fix by stopping watering, letting the soil dry, improving drainage, and repotting with fresh soil if the roots are black and mushy.

SIGNS OF UNDERWATERING: Dry, crispy brown leaf edges, drooping leaves that perk up after watering, soil pulling away from the pot edge, wrinkled leaves in succulents, and slow growth.

GENERAL SUNLIGHT RULES: Full sun means 6 or more hours of direct sunlight daily. Partial sun or bright indirect light means 3-6 hours or light that does not hit the leaves directly. Low light means a shaded spot away from windows. Leaves turning pale or leggy stems stretching toward light means too little light. Brown, scorched or bleached patches mean too much direct sun. Rotate pots a quarter turn every week for even growth.

SOIL BASICS: Most houseplants need a well-draining potting mix made of garden soil, compost and sand or perlite. Succulents and cacti need a sandy mix with extra sand or perlite. Herbs and vegetables prefer loamy soil rich in compost. Always use pots with drainage holes. Never use soil from a pond or heavy clay alone in pots.

FERTILIZER BASICS: Feed during the growing season (spring and summer) every 2-4 weeks with a diluted balanced liquid fertilizer. Reduce or stop feeding in winter. Organic options include compost, vermicompost, cow dung manure, banana peel compost and neem cake. Never fertilize a dry or stressed plant. Too much fertilizer causes white crust on the soil and burnt leaf tips.

REPOTTING: Repot when roots come out of the drainage hole, the plant dries out very quickly, or growth stops. Choose a pot only 2-5 cm wider than the old one. The best time is spring. Water lightly after repotting and keep the plant out of strong sun for a few days.

PRUNING: Remove dead, yellow and diseased leaves with clean scissors. Pinch the tips of herbs and leafy plants to make them bushy. Prune flowering plants after flowering. Always sterilize tools with alcohol to prevent spreading disease.

HUMIDITY: Tropical plants such as ferns, peace lily and orchids like humidity above 50 percent. Mist leaves, group plants together or place pots on a pebble tray with water. Succulents and cacti prefer dry air.

TEMPERATURE: Most houseplants grow well between 18 and 30 degrees Celsius. Keep plants away from air conditioner vents, heaters and cold drafts. Protect tender plants from frost.

CLEANING LEAVES: Wipe large leaves with a damp cloth every 2-4 weeks. Dust blocks sunlight and reduces photosynthesis.

WATERING SCHEDULE BY SEASON: Summer needs more frequent watering. Monsoon needs less watering and good drainage. Winter needs the least watering because growth slows down.

PART 2: INDIVIDUAL PLANT GUIDES
=====================================================

--- TULSI (Holy Basil, Ocimum tenuiflorum) ---
TULSI Watering: Water daily in summer and keep the soil moist but not soggy. In winter water every 2-3 days.
TULSI Sunlight: Needs 4-6 hours of direct sunlight daily.
TULSI Soil: Loamy, well-draining soil enriched with compost.
TULSI Maintenance: Pinch off flower buds to keep leaves growing. Prune regularly and protect from frost. Replace old woody plants every 2 years from seeds or cuttings.
TULSI Problems: Aphids and whiteflies, treated with neem oil spray. Leaf drop in cold weather. Fungal spots from overwatering.
TULSI Uses: Sacred plant in Indian homes. Leaves are used for herbal tea (tulsi kadha), added to drinking water and some dishes. Natural mosquito repellent around homes.
TULSI Medicinal: In Ayurveda, tulsi is traditionally used for coughs, colds, sore throat and general wellness, often as tea with ginger and honey. Reported antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties; more clinical research is needed.
TULSI Safety: Generally safe in food amounts. Large amounts may affect blood sugar and blood clotting. People on diabetes or blood-thinner medicines, and pregnant women, should consult a doctor.

--- ALOE VERA ---
ALOE VERA Watering: Water deeply every 2-3 weeks and let the soil dry fully in between. Reduce in winter.
ALOE VERA Sunlight: Bright light, at least 6 hours of indirect or filtered sunlight. Harsh direct afternoon sun turns leaves brown.
ALOE VERA Soil: Sandy, fast-draining succulent mix.
ALOE VERA Maintenance: Remove dead outer leaves. Repot when baby plants (pups) crowd the pot. Fertilize once in spring.
ALOE VERA Problems: Thin curling leaves mean thirst. Soft brown leaves mean overwatering and root rot.
ALOE VERA Uses: Gel is used in skin care, hair masks, moisturisers and juices. Easy decorative succulent.
ALOE VERA Medicinal: Traditionally used to soothe minor burns, sunburn and dry skin; small amounts of gel are used in some traditional practices for digestion.
ALOE VERA Safety: The yellow latex under the leaf skin is a strong laxative; avoid it. Toxic to dogs and cats. Do a patch test before applying to skin. Pregnant women should consult a doctor.

--- SNAKE PLANT (Sansevieria, Dracaena trifasciata) ---
SNAKE PLANT Watering: Every 2-3 weeks. Let the soil dry completely. In winter water once a month.
SNAKE PLANT Sunlight: Tolerates low light but grows best in bright indirect light. Handles a few hours of morning sun.
SNAKE PLANT Soil: Well-draining cactus or succulent mix with a pot that has drainage holes.
SNAKE PLANT Maintenance: Wipe leaves monthly. Fertilize lightly in spring and summer. Repot every 2-3 years. Propagate by leaf cuttings or division.
SNAKE PLANT Problems: Soft drooping leaves mean overwatering. Wrinkled leaves mean underwatering. Mealybugs can be removed with a cotton swab dipped in rubbing alcohol.
SNAKE PLANT Uses: Popular, nearly indestructible indoor ornamental plant that helps filter indoor air pollutants.
SNAKE PLANT Safety: Mildly toxic to pets and people if eaten; can cause nausea and vomiting.

--- MONEY PLANT (Pothos, Epipremnum aureum) ---
MONEY PLANT Watering: Water when the top 2-3 cm of soil is dry, about once a week. Can also grow in a jar of water; change water every 1-2 weeks.
MONEY PLANT Sunlight: Medium to bright indirect light. Tolerates low light but variegation fades.
MONEY PLANT Soil: Regular potting mix with good drainage.
MONEY PLANT Maintenance: Trim long vines to encourage bushy growth. Feed monthly in growing season with balanced liquid fertilizer. Propagate easily from stem cuttings in water.
MONEY PLANT Problems: Yellow leaves mean overwatering. Brown crispy tips mean low humidity or underwatering.
MONEY PLANT Uses: Popular hanging or climbing ornamental plant for homes and offices.
MONEY PLANT Safety: Toxic to pets and people if eaten because of calcium oxalate crystals; sap may irritate skin.

--- NEEM (Azadirachta indica) ---
NEEM Watering: Young trees need regular watering; mature trees are drought tolerant.
NEEM Sunlight: Full direct sunlight, 6-8 hours.
NEEM Soil: Well-draining sandy or loamy soil.
NEEM Maintenance: Prune for shape. Fallen leaves make good compost.
NEEM Uses: Large shade tree. Neem oil spray is a natural garden pesticide. Twigs are traditionally used as toothbrushes.
NEEM Medicinal: Traditionally used in Ayurveda for skin problems, dental care and as a blood purifier. Reported antibacterial and antifungal properties.
NEEM Safety: Neem oil must never be swallowed, especially by children. Avoid during pregnancy.

--- SPIDER PLANT (Chlorophytum comosum) ---
SPIDER PLANT Watering: Water once a week; keep the soil lightly moist but never soggy. Use filtered water if leaf tips turn brown.
SPIDER PLANT Sunlight: Bright indirect light. Tolerates some shade. Avoid harsh direct sun.
SPIDER PLANT Soil: Regular well-draining potting mix.
SPIDER PLANT Maintenance: Remove baby plantlets (spiderettes) and pot them to make new plants. Fertilize monthly in the growing season.
SPIDER PLANT Problems: Brown tips from fluoride or chlorine in tap water, or dry air.
SPIDER PLANT Uses: Hanging ornamental plant known for air purification.
SPIDER PLANT Safety: Non-toxic to cats and dogs, making it a pet-friendly choice.

--- PEACE LILY (Spathiphyllum) ---
PEACE LILY Watering: Water once a week when the top soil is dry. It droops dramatically when thirsty and recovers quickly after watering.
PEACE LILY Sunlight: Low to medium indirect light. Direct sun burns leaves.
PEACE LILY Soil: Rich, well-draining potting mix.
PEACE LILY Maintenance: Wipe leaves, mist occasionally, remove spent flowers and yellow leaves. Fertilize every 6-8 weeks.
PEACE LILY Problems: Brown leaf tips from dry air or fluoride in water. Yellow leaves from overwatering. No flowers means too little light.
PEACE LILY Uses: Ornamental plant with white flowers; helps improve indoor air quality.
PEACE LILY Safety: Toxic to cats, dogs and children if eaten; irritates mouth and throat.

--- ROSE ---
ROSE Watering: Water deeply 2-3 times a week in summer, less in winter. Water at the base, not on the leaves, to avoid fungus.
ROSE Sunlight: Full sun, at least 6 hours a day.
ROSE Soil: Rich, loamy, well-draining soil with compost or manure. Slightly acidic pH.
ROSE Maintenance: Prune in winter or late winter to shape and remove dead wood. Remove faded flowers (deadheading) to encourage more blooms. Feed every 4-6 weeks with rose fertilizer.
ROSE Problems: Aphids, black spot and powdery mildew. Spray neem oil or soap solution and improve air circulation.
ROSE Uses: Ornamental flowers, perfume, rose water and gulkand. Petals are used in teas and sweets.
ROSE Medicinal: Rose water is traditionally used as a skin toner and for soothing tired eyes; rose petals are used in some traditional cooling preparations.
ROSE Safety: Use only pesticide-free roses for eating. Possible allergy in sensitive people.

--- HIBISCUS (Hibiscus rosa-sinensis) ---
HIBISCUS Watering: Keep the soil evenly moist; water daily in hot summer, less in winter.
HIBISCUS Sunlight: Full sun, at least 5-6 hours, for plenty of flowers.
HIBISCUS Soil: Rich, well-draining soil with compost.
HIBISCUS Maintenance: Prune in spring to keep it bushy. Feed every 2-3 weeks in the growing season with a potassium-rich fertilizer.
HIBISCUS Problems: Bud drop from irregular watering or low light. Aphids, mealybugs and whiteflies.
HIBISCUS Uses: Ornamental flowers used in worship. Flowers and leaves are used in hair oils and masks. Dried flowers make herbal tea.
HIBISCUS Medicinal: Traditionally used in hair care for hair fall and dandruff, and hibiscus tea is traditionally used for cooling effects. Roselle hibiscus tea is reported to affect blood pressure.
HIBISCUS Safety: People on blood-pressure medicine and pregnant women should consult a doctor before drinking hibiscus tea regularly.

--- JASMINE (Jasminum) ---
JASMINE Watering: Water regularly, about 2-3 times a week in summer; let the top soil dry slightly between waterings.
JASMINE Sunlight: Full sun to partial shade, 4-6 hours.
JASMINE Soil: Loamy, well-draining, slightly acidic soil.
JASMINE Maintenance: Prune after flowering. Provide support for climbing types. Fertilize every month during growth.
JASMINE Problems: Aphids, spider mites and leaf yellowing from poor drainage.
JASMINE Uses: Fragrant flowers used in garlands, perfumes, hair decoration and jasmine tea.
JASMINE Medicinal: Jasmine tea and oil are traditionally used for relaxation and stress relief through aroma.

--- MINT (Pudina) ---
MINT Watering: Keep the soil consistently moist; water every 1-2 days in summer.
MINT Sunlight: Partial shade to 4 hours of sun. Too much hot sun wilts leaves.
MINT Soil: Rich, moist, well-draining soil. Grow in its own pot because it spreads aggressively.
MINT Maintenance: Pinch tips often for bushy growth and harvest regularly. Cut flowers off.
MINT Problems: Rust fungus, aphids and spider mites. Remove affected leaves.
MINT Uses: Used in chutneys, drinks, tea, raita and as a breath freshener. Repels mosquitoes and ants.
MINT Medicinal: Traditionally used to ease indigestion, gas and nausea, and mint tea is used for refreshing the throat.
MINT Safety: Peppermint oil should not be applied on the face of infants. People with acid reflux may find mint worsens symptoms.

--- CURRY LEAF (Murraya koenigii) ---
CURRY LEAF Watering: Water when the top soil is dry, about 2-3 times a week in summer. Avoid waterlogging.
CURRY LEAF Sunlight: Full sun, at least 5-6 hours.
CURRY LEAF Soil: Well-draining loamy soil with compost.
CURRY LEAF Maintenance: Prune tips to encourage branching. Feed with diluted buttermilk, compost or cow dung manure every 3-4 weeks. Harvest leaves from the top.
CURRY LEAF Problems: Yellow leaves from nutrient deficiency or overwatering. Aphids and scale insects.
CURRY LEAF Uses: Essential flavouring in Indian cooking, used in tempering, chutneys and hair oil.
CURRY LEAF Medicinal: Traditionally used for digestion and hair care. Reported to be rich in antioxidants.

--- LEMON (Citrus limon) ---
LEMON Watering: Water deeply when the top 3-5 cm of soil is dry; roughly twice a week in summer.
LEMON Sunlight: Full sun, 6-8 hours.
LEMON Soil: Well-draining, slightly acidic loamy soil.
LEMON Maintenance: Feed every month with citrus fertilizer. Prune dead branches. Mulch around the base.
LEMON Problems: Citrus leaf miner, aphids and scale. Yellow leaves mean iron or nitrogen deficiency.
LEMON Uses: Fruit used in food, drinks, pickles and cleaning. Leaves and flowers are fragrant.
LEMON Medicinal: Lemon water with honey is a traditional home remedy for sore throat and refreshment; lemon is a source of vitamin C.
LEMON Safety: Acidic juice can harm tooth enamel; rinse the mouth after drinking.

--- CHILLI (Capsicum) ---
CHILLI Watering: Water regularly, keeping the soil moist but not waterlogged. Water more during flowering and fruiting.
CHILLI Sunlight: Full sun, at least 6 hours.
CHILLI Soil: Loamy, well-draining, rich in organic matter.
CHILLI Maintenance: Feed every 2-3 weeks with a balanced fertilizer. Pinch growing tips to get bushy plants. Harvest regularly for more fruit.
CHILLI Problems: Leaf curl from mites or thrips. Flower drop in extreme heat. Aphids.
CHILLI Uses: Fruits used as spice in cooking and pickles.
CHILLI Safety: Wash hands after handling chillies; avoid touching eyes.

--- TOMATO ---
TOMATO Watering: Water deeply and regularly, about every 2 days in summer. Keep watering uniform to avoid fruit cracking.
TOMATO Sunlight: Full sun, 6-8 hours.
TOMATO Soil: Rich loamy soil with compost, slightly acidic.
TOMATO Maintenance: Stake or cage the plants. Remove lower leaves. Feed every 2 weeks once flowering starts.
TOMATO Problems: Blossom end rot from calcium deficiency and uneven watering. Early blight, whiteflies and fruit borers.
TOMATO Uses: Fruit used in cooking, salads and sauces.
TOMATO Safety: Leaves and stems of the tomato plant are toxic to pets if eaten.

--- MARIGOLD (Genda) ---
MARIGOLD Watering: Water every 2-3 days; avoid wetting flowers and leaves.
MARIGOLD Sunlight: Full sun, at least 6 hours.
MARIGOLD Soil: Well-draining ordinary garden soil.
MARIGOLD Maintenance: Remove faded flowers (deadhead) to get continuous blooms. Needs little fertilizer.
MARIGOLD Problems: Spider mites in dry weather. Fungal disease from overhead watering.
MARIGOLD Uses: Flowers used in garlands and festivals, natural dye and as a companion plant that repels many garden pests.
MARIGOLD Medicinal: Marigold (calendula) petals are traditionally used in creams for minor skin irritation.

--- ARECA PALM ---
ARECA PALM Watering: Keep the soil lightly moist; water about twice a week and less in winter.
ARECA PALM Sunlight: Bright indirect light; avoid hot direct sun.
ARECA PALM Soil: Well-draining potting mix with compost.
ARECA PALM Maintenance: Mist leaves often, remove brown fronds at the base, fertilize monthly in summer.
ARECA PALM Problems: Brown tips from dry air or fluoride in water. Spider mites.
ARECA PALM Uses: Popular indoor decorative palm that adds humidity to rooms.
ARECA PALM Safety: Non-toxic to cats and dogs.

--- RUBBER PLANT (Ficus elastica) ---
RUBBER PLANT Watering: Water when the top 3-5 cm of soil is dry, about every 1-2 weeks.
RUBBER PLANT Sunlight: Bright indirect light.
RUBBER PLANT Soil: Well-draining potting mix.
RUBBER PLANT Maintenance: Wipe leaves regularly. Rotate pot for even growth. Prune to control height.
RUBBER PLANT Problems: Leaf drop from overwatering, drafts or low light.
RUBBER PLANT Uses: Large ornamental indoor plant that helps filter air.
RUBBER PLANT Safety: The milky sap is toxic and can irritate skin; toxic to pets.

--- JADE PLANT (Crassula ovata) ---
JADE PLANT Watering: Water every 2-3 weeks and only when the soil is completely dry.
JADE PLANT Sunlight: Bright light with 4-6 hours of sun.
JADE PLANT Soil: Sandy succulent mix.
JADE PLANT Maintenance: Prune for shape. Repot every 2-3 years. Propagate easily from leaf or stem cuttings.
JADE PLANT Problems: Soft shriveled leaves from overwatering. Leaf drop in low light.
JADE PLANT Uses: Considered a lucky plant and a long-living ornamental succulent.
JADE PLANT Safety: Mildly toxic to pets and people if eaten.

--- CACTUS ---
CACTUS Watering: Water every 3-4 weeks in summer and almost not at all in winter. Soil must be fully dry.
CACTUS Sunlight: Full sun or very bright light, at least 6 hours.
CACTUS Soil: Cactus mix with plenty of sand and perlite.
CACTUS Maintenance: Use a small pot with drainage. Feed lightly once or twice in the growing season. Handle with thick gloves.
CACTUS Problems: Rotting base from overwatering. Stretching and thinning from lack of light.
CACTUS Uses: Low-maintenance decorative plants; some species such as prickly pear produce edible fruit.

--- ASHWAGANDHA (Withania somnifera) ---
ASHWAGANDHA Watering: Water lightly every 3-4 days; it is drought tolerant and dislikes waterlogging.
ASHWAGANDHA Sunlight: Full sun, 6 hours or more.
ASHWAGANDHA Soil: Sandy loam, well-draining soil.
ASHWAGANDHA Maintenance: Needs little fertilizer. Roots are harvested after 5-6 months.
ASHWAGANDHA Medicinal: In Ayurveda, the root is traditionally used as an adaptogen for stress, energy and sleep. Some studies suggest it may reduce stress; more research is needed.
ASHWAGANDHA Safety: Not recommended during pregnancy. People with thyroid problems, autoimmune disease or on sedatives should consult a doctor.

--- GILOY (Tinospora cordifolia) ---
GILOY Watering: Water 2-3 times a week; keep the soil moist but not soggy.
GILOY Sunlight: Partial to full sun with a support to climb on.
GILOY Soil: Well-draining loamy soil.
GILOY Maintenance: Grows easily from stem cuttings. Prune to control spread.
GILOY Medicinal: In Ayurveda, giloy stem is traditionally used to support immunity and in fever remedies.
GILOY Safety: People with autoimmune conditions, diabetes, or those about to have surgery should consult a doctor. Long-term high use has been linked in some reports to liver problems.

--- LEMONGRASS ---
LEMONGRASS Watering: Water regularly; keep the soil moist in summer and reduce in winter.
LEMONGRASS Sunlight: Full sun, 6-8 hours.
LEMONGRASS Soil: Loamy, well-draining soil. Grows well in large pots.
LEMONGRASS Maintenance: Harvest outer stalks at the base. Divide clumps every 2 years. Protect from frost.
LEMONGRASS Uses: Used in tea, soups and curries; the aroma repels mosquitoes.
LEMONGRASS Medicinal: Lemongrass tea is traditionally used for digestion and refreshing the body.

--- CORIANDER (Dhania) ---
CORIANDER Watering: Keep the soil lightly moist; water daily in hot weather using a gentle spray.
CORIANDER Sunlight: Partial sun, 4-5 hours; it bolts quickly in extreme heat.
CORIANDER Soil: Loose, well-draining loamy soil.
CORIANDER Maintenance: Sow seeds directly every 2-3 weeks for continuous harvest. Harvest leaves in 3-4 weeks. Best grown in cooler months.
CORIANDER Uses: Leaves are used as garnish and in chutneys; seeds are used as a spice.
CORIANDER Medicinal: Coriander seed water is traditionally used to help digestion.

--- LUCKY BAMBOO (Dracaena sanderiana) ---
LUCKY BAMBOO Watering: Keep roots covered with water if grown in a vase; change the water every 1-2 weeks. In soil keep it lightly moist.
LUCKY BAMBOO Sunlight: Indirect light; direct sun burns leaves.
LUCKY BAMBOO Maintenance: Use filtered or distilled water. Add a drop of liquid fertilizer once a month. Trim yellow leaves.
LUCKY BAMBOO Problems: Yellow stalks from too much sun, fertilizer or chlorine in water.
LUCKY BAMBOO Uses: Considered a good luck plant, popular for desks and homes.
LUCKY BAMBOO Safety: Toxic to cats and dogs.

--- BOSTON FERN ---
BOSTON FERN Watering: Keep the soil constantly moist but not waterlogged; water 2-3 times a week.
BOSTON FERN Sunlight: Bright indirect light or partial shade.
BOSTON FERN Soil: Rich, moisture-holding, well-draining potting mix.
BOSTON FERN Maintenance: Mist daily, keep in humid places such as bathrooms. Trim brown fronds. Feed monthly in growing season.
BOSTON FERN Problems: Brown fronds from dry air or too little water.
BOSTON FERN Uses: Lush decorative hanging plant that adds humidity indoors.
BOSTON FERN Safety: Non-toxic to cats and dogs.

--- ORCHID (Phalaenopsis) ---
ORCHID Watering: Water once a week by soaking the roots for a few minutes, then drain well. Never leave water in the crown.
ORCHID Sunlight: Bright indirect light. Direct sun burns the leaves.
ORCHID Soil: Use orchid bark mix, not normal soil.
ORCHID Maintenance: Feed with diluted orchid fertilizer every 2 weeks. After flowers fall, cut the spike above a node to try for re-bloom.
ORCHID Problems: Root rot from overwatering. Wrinkled leaves from underwatering.
ORCHID Safety: Phalaenopsis orchids are non-toxic to cats and dogs.

--- TURMERIC (Haldi) ---
TURMERIC Watering: Water regularly in monsoon and summer, keep the soil moist but not waterlogged. Stop watering as the leaves dry out.
TURMERIC Sunlight: Partial sun to full sun.
TURMERIC Soil: Rich, loose, well-draining soil. Plant rhizome pieces 5 cm deep.
TURMERIC Maintenance: Harvest after 8-9 months when leaves turn yellow and dry.
TURMERIC Uses: Spice, natural dye and an ingredient in many traditional foods.
TURMERIC Medicinal: Turmeric (haldi) with milk is a traditional home remedy for cold and for general wellness. Curcumin in turmeric is studied for anti-inflammatory properties.
TURMERIC Safety: Large supplement doses can interact with blood thinners and may upset the stomach. Consult a doctor if you have gallbladder problems.

--- GINGER (Adrak) ---
GINGER Watering: Keep the soil lightly moist; water 2-3 times a week and avoid waterlogging.
GINGER Sunlight: Partial shade or morning sun.
GINGER Soil: Loose, rich, well-draining soil. Plant fresh rhizome pieces with buds facing up.
GINGER Maintenance: Mulch the soil. Harvest after 8-10 months.
GINGER Uses: Used in cooking, tea, pickles and sweets.
GINGER Medicinal: Ginger tea is traditionally used for nausea, indigestion, cold and sore throat.
GINGER Safety: Large amounts may cause heartburn and may increase bleeding risk with blood thinners.

--- BRAHMI (Bacopa monnieri) ---
BRAHMI Watering: Likes wet soil; water daily and keep the soil constantly moist.
BRAHMI Sunlight: Partial to full sun.
BRAHMI Soil: Moist, loamy soil. Can be grown in shallow trays with water.
BRAHMI Maintenance: Trim regularly to keep it spreading. Propagate by stem cuttings.
BRAHMI Medicinal: In Ayurveda, brahmi is traditionally used to support memory, focus and calmness.
BRAHMI Safety: May cause stomach upset or drowsiness. Consult a doctor if pregnant or on medication.

--- LAVENDER ---
LAVENDER Watering: Water lightly once a week; let the soil dry between waterings.
LAVENDER Sunlight: Full sun, 6-8 hours.
LAVENDER Soil: Sandy, alkaline, very well-draining soil.
LAVENDER Maintenance: Prune after flowering. Avoid fertilizing heavily.
LAVENDER Problems: Root rot in humid, wet conditions.
LAVENDER Uses: Fragrant flowers used for sachets, essential oil and tea.
LAVENDER Medicinal: Lavender scent is traditionally used to promote relaxation and sleep.
LAVENDER Safety: Toxic to cats and dogs in large amounts. Do not swallow lavender essential oil.

--- MONSTERA (Swiss Cheese Plant) ---
MONSTERA Watering: Water every 1-2 weeks when the top 5 cm of soil is dry.
MONSTERA Sunlight: Bright indirect light.
MONSTERA Soil: Chunky, well-draining potting mix.
MONSTERA Maintenance: Provide a moss pole to climb. Wipe leaves and fertilize monthly in the growing season.
MONSTERA Problems: Yellow leaves from overwatering. No leaf holes means too little light.
MONSTERA Safety: Toxic to pets and people if eaten.

--- ZZ PLANT (Zamioculcas zamiifolia) ---
ZZ PLANT Watering: Water every 2-3 weeks and let the soil dry completely.
ZZ PLANT Sunlight: Low to bright indirect light; very tolerant.
ZZ PLANT Soil: Well-draining potting mix.
ZZ PLANT Maintenance: Wipe leaves occasionally and feed 2-3 times a year.
ZZ PLANT Problems: Yellowing from overwatering; rotting rhizomes.
ZZ PLANT Safety: Toxic if eaten; wash hands after handling.

--- BOUGAINVILLEA ---
BOUGAINVILLEA Watering: Water deeply but infrequently, only when the soil is dry. Too much water reduces flowering.
BOUGAINVILLEA Sunlight: Full sun, at least 6 hours, for best colour.
BOUGAINVILLEA Soil: Well-draining, slightly poor soil.
BOUGAINVILLEA Maintenance: Prune after each bloom cycle. Use fertilizer low in nitrogen and rich in phosphorus. Wear gloves because of thorns.

=====================================================
PART 3: PESTS AND DISEASES
=====================================================

APHIDS: Tiny green or black insects on new shoots. Wash off with water, spray neem oil or soap solution (1 teaspoon mild soap in 1 litre water).

MEALYBUGS: White cottony patches on stems and leaf joints. Dab with a cotton swab dipped in rubbing alcohol; spray neem oil weekly.

SPIDER MITES: Fine webs and speckled yellow leaves, common in dry air. Increase humidity, wash leaves, spray neem oil.

WHITEFLIES: Tiny white flying insects under leaves. Use yellow sticky traps and neem oil spray.

SCALE INSECTS: Brown bumps on stems and leaves. Scrape off gently and treat with neem oil or alcohol.

FUNGUS GNATS: Small flies around the soil caused by constantly wet soil. Let the soil dry, add sticky traps, and top soil with sand.

POWDERY MILDEW: White powder on leaves. Improve air circulation, remove affected leaves, spray diluted baking soda solution or neem oil.

ROOT ROT: Black, mushy roots and wilting despite wet soil. Remove the plant, cut rotten roots, repot in fresh dry mix and water less.

LEAF SPOT: Brown or black spots with yellow edges. Remove affected leaves, avoid wetting leaves, spray neem oil.

NEEM OIL SPRAY RECIPE: Mix 5 ml neem oil and 2-3 drops of liquid soap in 1 litre of water. Spray in the evening on both sides of the leaves every 7 days. Test on one leaf first.


=====================================================
PART 4: SEASONAL CARE
=====================================================

SUMMER CARE: Water more often, in the early morning. Move delicate plants away from hot, direct afternoon sun. Mulch the soil to keep it cool. Watch for spider mites.

MONSOON CARE: Reduce watering, ensure drainage, move pots out of heavy rain, and watch for fungal diseases and snails. Good season for planting and propagation.

WINTER CARE: Water less, stop or reduce fertilizer, keep tender plants away from cold drafts, and protect outdoor plants from frost with a cloth cover.

SPRING CARE: Best time to repot, prune and start fertilizing. Start new seeds and cuttings.


=====================================================
PART 5: PROPAGATION AND COMPOSTING
=====================================================

PROPAGATION BY CUTTINGS: Cut a healthy stem below a node, remove lower leaves and place in water or moist soil. Works well for money plant, tulsi, mint, rose, jade and giloy.

PROPAGATION BY DIVISION: Separate clumps with roots and replant. Works for snake plant, spider plant, aloe vera and lemongrass.

HOMEMADE COMPOST: Mix kitchen waste such as vegetable peels, tea leaves and dry leaves with soil. Keep slightly moist and turn weekly. Ready in 6-8 weeks. Avoid meat, oil and dairy.

BANANA PEEL FERTILIZER: Chop peels and bury them in the soil, or soak in water for 2 days and use as diluted liquid feed. A source of potassium for flowering plants.


=====================================================
PART 6: QUICK TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE
=====================================================

YELLOW LEAVES: Most often overwatering. Other causes: low light, nutrient deficiency, natural aging of old leaves.

BROWN LEAF TIPS: Dry air, fluoride or chlorine in water, underwatering or fertilizer burn.

DROOPING OR WILTING: Underwatering or overwatering; check the soil first. Also heat stress or root damage.

LEGGY STRETCHED GROWTH: Not enough light. Move to a brighter spot and prune.

NO FLOWERS: Too little sunlight, too much nitrogen fertilizer or wrong pruning time.

LEAF DROP: Sudden change in light, temperature or watering; cold drafts; overwatering.

WHITE CRUST ON SOIL: Fertilizer or mineral salts build-up; flush the soil with plenty of water.

BROWN SPOTS ON LEAVES: Sunburn, fungal infection or water drops on leaves in strong sun.

PETS AND CHILDREN: Keep toxic plants such as aloe vera, snake plant, money plant, peace lily, rubber plant, monstera, ZZ plant, lucky bamboo and lavender (in large amounts) out of reach. Pet-friendly choices include spider plant, areca palm, Boston fern and orchids.

BEST LOW-MAINTENANCE PLANTS FOR BEGINNERS: Snake plant, ZZ plant, money plant, aloe vera, jade plant and spider plant.

BEST PLANTS FOR LOW LIGHT: Snake plant, ZZ plant, money plant, peace lily and Boston fern.

BEST PLANTS FOR FULL SUN BALCONY: Tulsi, rose, hibiscus, marigold, bougainvillea, curry leaf, lemon and cactus.

BEST HERBAL AND MEDICINAL PLANTS TO GROW AT HOME: Tulsi, aloe vera, mint, ashwagandha, giloy, brahmi, lemongrass, turmeric and ginger.
